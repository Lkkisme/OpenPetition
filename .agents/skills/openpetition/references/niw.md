# EB-2 NIW: National Interest Waiver

Governing authorities:
- **Statute:** INA § 203(b)(2)(B), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(2)(B)
- **Regulation:** [8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-204/subpart-A/section-204.5)
- **Controlling Precedent:** [*Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016)](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/page/file/920996/dl); *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010)
- **Policy Guidance:** [USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 6, Part F, Chapter 5](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-5) (incorporating PA-2022-02 and the Jan. 15, 2025 PA-2025-03 update)

---

## 1. Standard of Proof & Two-Step Analytical Structure

Under *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010), an EB-2 NIW petition is adjudicated under the **preponderance of the evidence ("more likely than not" / >50%)** standard.

Keep two strict boundaries throughout every NIW assessment and brief:
1. **Strict Separation Between Step 1 (EB-2 Eligibility) and Step 2 (The Three *Dhanasar* Prongs).**
2. **No EB-1A Terminology Bleed:** Never use EB-1A statutory language (*"extraordinary ability"*, *"sustained national or international acclaim"*, *"small percentage at the very top of the field"*) in an NIW brief—using EB-1A terms invites USCIS officers to silently apply a higher standard than *Dhanasar* permits.

---

## 2. Step 1: Threshold EB-2 Classification & Profession Nexus (`6 USCIS-PM F.5`, Jan. 15, 2025 Update)

Under the **Jan. 15, 2025 USCIS Policy Manual update (`PA-2025-03`)**, officers apply heightened front-end scrutiny to verify both underlying EB-2 qualification **and** its direct nexus to the Proposed Endeavor:

### Route A: Advanced Degree Professional (`8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(2)`)
- **Qualifying Credential:**
  - **U.S. Master's or Higher (or Foreign Equivalent):** Official diploma + complete transcripts (plus a formal credential evaluation for foreign degrees), **or**
  - **Baccalaureate + 5 Years Progressive Post-Baccalaureate Experience:** Official bachelor's diploma + transcripts accompanied by detailed employer letters verifying at least 5 years of progressive post-degree experience **in the specialty**.
- **Mandatory Profession & Specialty Nexus (2025 Update):**
  - Prove that (1) the Proposed Endeavor actually requires a member of the **professions** holding an advanced degree, and (2) the applicant's advanced degree or 5-year progressive experience is in the **exact specialty** required by the endeavor.

### Route B: Exceptional Ability in the Sciences, Arts, or Business (`8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(3)(ii)`)
- **Part 1:** Satisfy at least **three (3) of the six (6) criteria**: (A) official academic record in the area, (B) 10+ years of full-time experience, (C) professional license/certification, (D) salary/remuneration demonstrating exceptional ability, (E) membership in professional associations, (F) recognition for achievements and significant contributions by peers, government entities, or professional/business organizations.
- **Part 2 + Nexus:** Demonstrate a degree of expertise significantly above that ordinarily encountered, and prove that the area of exceptional ability is **directly related** to the Proposed Endeavor.

---

## 3. Step 2: Defining the Proposed Endeavor

The #1 cause of NIW RFEs under the 2025 Policy Manual is defining the endeavor as a generic job title (*"working as a Senior Software Engineer"*) or a broad discipline (*"conducting oncology research"*).

State the **Proposed Endeavor** in a dedicated section before Prong 1, specifying:
1. **The Specific Technical / Practical Problem & Objective:** What concrete architecture, therapeutic, diagnostic, algorithm, infrastructure, or platform the applicant will build or investigate in the United States.
2. **Execution Vehicle & 1–3 Year Roadmap:** Where and how the work will be carried out (research lab, engineering division, clinical center, or startup venture) with concrete near-, medium-, and long-term milestones.
3. **Mechanism of Broad Dissemination Beyond One Employer:** How the endeavor's outputs (open-source releases, peer-reviewed publications, patents, industry standards, scalable platforms, clinical protocols, or cross-sector deployments) diffuse benefits across the field or nation rather than remaining locked inside a single employer or local client base.

---

## 4. Step 3: The Three *Dhanasar* Prongs

### Prong 1: Substantial Merit and National Importance (Endeavor-Focused)

Keep Prong 1 strictly focused on the **Proposed Endeavor** (save the applicant's personal track record for Prong 2), and address **Substantial Merit** and **National Importance** under separate subheadings:

1. **Substantial Merit:**
   - Prove the endeavor's intrinsic value to science, technology, engineering, healthcare, economic competitiveness, cybersecurity, clean energy, education, or national security.
   - Under *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889, pure or foundational scientific research qualifies without needing immediate quantifiable economic return.

2. **National Importance (incorporating Jan. 15, 2025 `PA-2025-03` Rules):**
   - **What NOT to argue:** Do **not** rely on generic "national labor shortage" arguments (USCIS 2025 guidance explicitly states labor shortages are addressed by PERM, not Prong 1), and do **not** rely solely on the size of the applicant's employer or general industry statistics.
   - **What TO prove:** Connect the *specific outputs* of the applicant's endeavor to field-wide technological progress, U.S. national priorities, or broad societal/economic ripple effects:
     - **STEM & Critical and Emerging Technologies (`6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(2)`):** Show how the specific endeavor advances a critical technology domain (AI, semiconductors, quantum, biotech, clean energy, cybersecurity, advanced manufacturing, autonomous systems) with concrete technical mechanisms rather than buzzwords.
     - **Field-Wide Dissemination:** Show how other U.S. researchers, engineers, clinicians, or enterprises will use the endeavor's published findings, open-source tools, datasets, patents, or standards.
     - **Economic / Clinical / Regional Multiplier:** Quantify downstream impact using authoritative U.S. federal agency data (see Section 5 below).

### Prong 2: Well Positioned to Advance the Proposed Endeavor (Person-Focused)

Evaluate the **applicant personally** across *Dhanasar*'s four factor groups (ultimate success need not be guaranteed):

1. **Education, Skills, Knowledge, and Record of Success in Related Efforts:**
   - Directly link each prior achievement (papers, **strictly independent citations**, patents, production systems, clinical outcomes, awards, peer-review service) to a concrete requirement of the Proposed Endeavor (*"Because Dr. X previously solved bottleneck A as shown in `[Exhibit B-2]`, they are uniquely positioned to execute Milestone 1 of the Proposed Endeavor"*).
   - For **Advanced STEM Degree (especially Ph.D.) holders**, cite `6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(2)`, which recognizes that an advanced STEM degree tied to the endeavor is an especially positive factor under Prong 2.
2. **Model or Plan for Future Activities:**
   - Reference the detailed 1–3 year roadmap in the applicant's signed Personal Statement (`final/02-personal-statement.md`).
3. **Progress Towards Achieving the Proposed Endeavor:**
   - Document existing prototypes, preliminary data, active codebases, deployed systems, pending manuscripts/patents, or ongoing collaborations.
4. **Interest of Potential Customers, Users, Investors, Government Agencies, or Relevant Entities:**
   - Independent citations and substantive reliance (`Semantic Scholar` `Method`/`Result` contexts), federal/institutional grants (NSF, NIH, DOE, DARPA, DOD, SBIR/STTR), letters from U.S. government/FFRDC stakeholders or independent domain experts, customer contracts/LOIs, or venture/angel backing.
   - **Entrepreneur Specifics (`6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3)`, Jan. 2025 Update):** Self-authored business projections alone are insufficient; anchor founder cases with executed contracts/LOIs, VC/angel/accelerator funding, SBIR/STTR grants, issued/pending IP, or documented user traction.

### Prong 3: On Balance, Beneficial to the United States to Waive the Job Offer and Labor Certification Requirements

Under *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 890–91, the applicant is **not** required to prove that U.S. workers are unavailable. Build a case-specific balancing argument around the USCIS Policy Manual factors:

1. **Structural Impracticality of PERM for the Specific Endeavor:**
   - Show why tying the applicant to a single employer's static minimum-qualifications PERM job description would restrict cross-institutional research, open-source/standards leadership, multi-domain innovation, or founder/self-directed work.
2. **Unique Qualifications Beyond a Minimally Qualified Worker:**
   - Explain why even assuming U.S. workers with the minimum degree exist, the applicant's proprietary methods, specialized track record, and active momentum cannot be replicated by a minimally qualified replacement.
3. **Urgency of the National Benefit & Cost of Delay:**
   - Demonstrate why delaying or interrupting the endeavor for the multi-year PERM process would harm time-sensitive U.S. technological competitiveness, clinical/public-health progress, or funded R&D milestones.

## 5. Primary U.S. Federal Statistical & Priority Sources for Prong 1

Always anchor Prong 1 National Importance in primary U.S. federal agency reports and statistics rather than blog posts or news summaries:

| Domain | Authoritative U.S. Federal Sources |
| :--- | :--- |
| **AI, Computing & Cybersecurity** | NIST (AI Risk Management Framework, Post-Quantum Cryptography), CISA Strategic Plans, NITRD / NSTC Critical and Emerging Technologies (CET) updates, NSF CISE reports |
| **Biomedicine, Healthcare & Public Health** | CDC National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS), NIH Strategic Plans & Institute priorities (NCI, NHLBI, NIAID), FDA Modernization / Digital Health guidance, ARPA-H mission areas |
| **Energy, Climate & Materials** | U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), DOE Quadrennial Technology Review, National Blueprint for Clean Energy / Grid Modernization, NREL technical reports |
| **Semiconductors, Hardware & Manufacturing** | NIST CHIPS for America reports, DOD Microelectronics Commons priorities, DOC Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) supply-chain data |
| **Economics, Labor & Infrastructure** | Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), Congressional Budget Office (CBO), DOT / FHWA infrastructure reports, USDA Economic Research Service |

**Mandatory Currency Rule:** Before citing any White House Executive Order or federal memorandum in Prong 1, verify that it is **currently in force** (for example, note that EO 14110 on AI was rescinded in January 2025; rely on active statutes such as the CHIPS and Science Act, statutory agency mandates, NIST/NIH/DOE technical roadmaps, or current NSTC/OSTP directives).
