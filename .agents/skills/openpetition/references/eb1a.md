# EB-1A: Alien of Extraordinary Ability

Governing authorities:
- **Statute:** INA § 203(b)(1)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(1)(A)
- **Regulation:** [8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-204/subpart-A/section-204.5)
- **Controlling Precedent:** *Kazarian v. USCIS*, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010); *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010)
- **Policy Guidance:** [USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 6, Part F, Chapter 2](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-2) (incorporating PA-2023-26 and PA-2024-24 updates)

## 1. Standard of Proof & Two-Step *Kazarian* Framework

### Standard of Proof: Preponderance of the Evidence (*Matter of Chawathe*)
Always open the Legal Standard section of an EB-1A brief (and any RFE response) with ***Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010)**:
- The petitioner need only establish that eligibility is **"more likely than not" (>50% probability)** true.
- Even if an officer has "some doubt," if the petitioner submits relevant, probative, and credible evidence showing the claim is "probably true," the burden of proof is met. Officers must evaluate evidence both individually and in the context of the totality of the record.

### Two-Step Adjudication (*Kazarian v. USCIS*)
Keep Step 1 and Step 2 strictly separated:
1. **Step 1 — Regulatory Threshold (`8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)–(4)`):**
   - Objective count of whether the record establishes **either** a one-time major international award **or** at least **three (3) of the ten (10) regulatory criteria** (or comparable evidence under `§ 204.5(h)(4)` when listed criteria do not readily apply to the occupation).
   - Under *Kazarian*, 596 F.3d at 1121–22, and *Buletini v. INS*, 860 F. Supp. 1222, 1230–34 (E.D. Mich. 1994), USCIS may **not** invent extra-regulatory requirements at Step 1 (such as demanding minimum citation counts under Criterion (vi) or requiring international recognition where the regulation states "nationally or internationally recognized").
2. **Step 2 — Final Merits Determination (Totality of the Evidence):**
   - Evaluates whether the totality of the evidence demonstrates: (a) **sustained national or international acclaim**, and (b) standing among the **small percentage at the very top of the field**, plus (c) clear evidence of **continuing work in the area of extraordinary ability in the United States** (`8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(5)`).

**Strategic Selection Rule:**
- Claim the **3 to 5 strongest, objectively provable criteria**, ordered by persuasive force. Padding weak criteria invites boilerplate RFEs.
- **Typical combinations by applicant archetype:**
  - **Academic / Scientist:** (iv) Judging + (v) Original Contributions + (vi) Scholarly Articles (+ (i) Prizes/Team Awards or (viii) Critical Role).
  - **Industry Engineer / Systems & AI Builder:** (v) Original Contributions (production architectures, patents, open-source adoption) + (viii) Leading/Critical Role + (ix) High Remuneration (+ (iv) Judging/PC/standards review or `§ 204.5(h)(4)` Comparable Evidence).
  - **Founder / Entrepreneur:** (v) Original Contributions + (viii) Leading Role + (i) Nationally Recognized Innovation/Grant Awards + (iii) Major/Trade Media or (ix) High Remuneration/Valued Equity.

## 2. Criterion-by-Criterion Proof & Recent Policy Manual Updates (6 USCIS-PM F.2)

1. **Lesser Nationally or Internationally Recognized Prizes or Awards — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)`**
   - **Oct. 2, 2024 Policy Manual Update (Team Awards Expressly Accepted):** USCIS explicitly confirms that **confirmatory evidence of the person's receipt of team awards** satisfies this criterion, provided the petitioner is a named recipient or documented member of the winning team (e.g., Best Paper Award co-author, national/international engineering competition team winner).
   - **Evidence to include:** Award certificate/announcement naming the applicant (or confirming team membership), official selection criteria, competitor pool scope (national/international, not limited to a single institution or employer), judging panel stature, and selectivity/media coverage.

2. **Membership in Associations Requiring Outstanding Achievements — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii)`**
   - **Oct. 2, 2024 Policy Manual Update (Past Memberships Expressly Accepted):** **Past memberships** in associations requiring outstanding achievements judged by recognized national or international experts satisfy this criterion; active/current dues-paying status at filing is not required.
   - **STEM / Professional Society Nuance:** Elevated fellow grades (e.g., IEEE Fellow, ACM Fellow, AAAI Fellow) where election requires outstanding achievements judged by an expert panel clearly qualify. Dues-based memberships or grades based solely on years of experience/education do not suffice unless accompanied by official bylaws and peer-review election proof (e.g., documented Sigma Xi Full Member or selective academy election).

3. **Published Material About the Applicant in Professional or Major Media — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii)`**
   - **Oct. 2, 2024 Policy Manual Update ("Value of Work" Requirement Removed):** USCIS **deleted** prior guidance suggesting the published article had to prove the *value* of the person's work. The article need only be **about the person, relating to the person's work in the field** (including articles about a team project where the applicant's significant role is named and discussed).
   - **Mandatory technical elements:** Every article exhibit must show **title, date, and author** (plus certified English translation if applicable) and independent proof of the outlet's circulation, readership/unique visitors, or standing as a professional/major trade publication or major media.

4. **Participation as a Judge of the Work of Others — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv)`**
   - **What to prove:** Actual **completed** participation, individually or on a panel, judging the work of others in the same or an allied field (peer review for journals/conferences, grant review panels, PhD dissertation committees for external candidates, technical standards/hackathon judging panels).
   - **Key RFE preemption:** Invitations alone fail Step 1; always attach completion confirmations (editor thank-you emails, Web of Science / ORCID verified records, conference PC badges/rosters + review completion proofs). Reserve venue prestige (`h5-index`, Impact Factor, acceptance rate) for Step 2 Final Merits.

5. **Original Scientific, Scholarly, Artistic, Athletic, or Business-Related Contributions of Major Significance — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v)`**
   - **What to prove:** (a) Originality, and (b) **major significance in the field** beyond the applicant's immediate employer or co-author circle (distinguishing *Amin v. Mayorkas*, 24 F.4th 383 (5th Cir. 2022)).
   - **Structure per contribution (`\subsubsection`):**
     `Prior Field Bottleneck -> Applicant's Specific Technical Innovation -> Independent External Adoption / Citations / Deployment -> Block Quotes from Independent Expert Letters -> Pinpoint Exhibit Callouts`.
   - **Evidence:** Highlighted excerpts from strictly independent citing papers (`Semantic Scholar` `Method`/`Result` contexts), OpenAlex year- and subfield-normalized citation percentiles (`Top 1%` / `Top 10%`, `fwci`), open-source adoption (stars, forks, production users), patent licensing/deployment, clinical guidelines, and detailed independent expert letters (*Chursov v. Miller*, 2019 WL 2085199 (S.D.N.Y. 2019)).

6. **Authorship of Scholarly Articles — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi)`**
   - **What to prove:** Authorship of peer-reviewed journal articles, peer-reviewed conference papers (expressly recognized for STEM/CS under 6 USCIS-PM F.2), or book chapters in professional/major trade publications.
   - **Key distinction:** Under *Kazarian*, USCIS cannot demand a specific citation count at Step 1 for Criterion (vi). Present citation percentiles and comparing per-paper citations against journal 2-year Impact Factors in Criterion (v) and Step 2.

7. **Display of Work at Artistic Exhibitions or Showcases — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii)`**
   - **Oct. 2, 2024 Policy Manual Update (Strictly Artistic):** Because the regulation specifies *"artistic"* exhibitions or showcases, non-artistic scientific posters or tech trade-show demos **cannot** be claimed directly under Criterion (vii). Present non-artistic showcases under **Comparable Evidence (`§ 204.5(h)(4)`)**, Criterion (v), or Step 2 Final Merits.

8. **Leading or Critical Role for Organizations or Establishments with a Distinguished Reputation — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii)`**
   - **Leading role:** Proven by title, org chart, and leadership duties (PI, Director, VP, Chief Architect, Founder).
   - **Critical role:** Proven by the decisive impact of the applicant's work on the organization's or division's outcomes (revenue, system reliability, flagship product launch, core research breakthrough)—independent of formal job title.
   - **Distinguished reputation:** Under 6 USCIS-PM F.2, a distinguished **department, division, or research lab** within an institution suffices. Prove eminence with independent rankings (QS, U.S. News, CSRankings, NSF HERD, NIH funding, Fortune 500, or tier-1 VC backing/scale).

9. **High Salary or Other Significantly High Remuneration — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix)`**
   - **What to prove:** Past or prospective compensation significantly above peers in the **same occupation (SOC code) and geographic area (MSA)**.
   - **Evidence:** W-2s (Box 1/5), tax returns, pay stubs, or executed offer letters, compared against **at least two** objective benchmarks: (1) **BLS OEWS 90th percentile** annual wage, (2) **DOL OFLC Level IV** wage, and (3) industry compensation surveys (e.g., Levels.fyi, Carta, Radford, AAMC) when total compensation includes substantial bonuses or vested equity.

10. **Commercial Successes in the Performing Arts — `8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(x)`**
    - Box office receipts, sales, or streaming volume relative to performing arts peers.

## 3. Federal Court Case Law Arsenal for Step 2 Final Merits & RFE Rebuttals

Use these federal court and AAO authorities to preempt or rebut improper USCIS objections:

| Case Authority | Core Holding & How to Use It in the Brief |
| :--- | :--- |
| ***Matter of Chawathe***, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) | **Binding AAO Precedent:** Standard of proof is **preponderance of the evidence (>50% "more likely than not")**. Even if the director has "some doubt," relevant, probative, and credible evidence satisfies the burden. |
| ***Kazarian v. USCIS***, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121–22 (9th Cir. 2010) | USCIS may **not** unilaterally impose novel substantive requirements onto the plain regulatory criteria at Step 1 (e.g., requiring citations to satisfy Authorship of Scholarly Articles). |
| ***Mukherji v. Miller***, No. 4:24-cv-03170, 2026 WL 895708 (D. Neb. Jan. 28, 2026) | **2026 Federal Court Precedent:** Vacated EB-1A denial and ordered approval where petitioner met $\ge 3$ criteria at Step 1, holding that USCIS cannot use an uncodified, subjective Step 2 "final merits" veto to negate satisfied regulatory criteria without articulated, objective standards. |
| ***Buletini v. INS***, 860 F. Supp. 1222, 1230–34 (E.D. Mich. 1994) | Once a petitioner satisfies at least three regulatory criteria, the agency abuses its discretion if it adds unwritten qualifiers or discounts qualifying evidence without specific, record-backed reasons. |
| ***Muni v. INS***, 891 F. Supp. 440, 444–45 (N.D. Ill. 1995) | An applicant need not be a household-name "superstar" or Nobel laureate to belong to the "small percentage at the very top of the field" under Step 2. |
| ***Grimson v. INS***, 934 F. Supp. 965 (N.D. Ill. 1996) | USCIS cannot dismiss achievements merely because the applicant operates in a specialized role or niche sub-discipline; comparison must be against peers in that specialized subfield. |
| ***Chursov v. Miller***, No. 18-cv-2886, 2019 WL 2085199 (S.D.N.Y. May 13, 2019) | Vacated EB-1A denial under Criterion (v); held that USCIS acted arbitrarily by nitpicking isolated sentences in expert recommendation letters while ignoring concrete descriptions of how the petitioner's methods and patents were used in the field. |
| ***Rubin v. Miller***, 478 F. Supp. 3d 499 (S.D.N.Y. 2020) | Vacated EB-1A denial; held that USCIS cannot invent extra-regulatory hurdles demanding that scientific contributions already be commercialized or generate general media fame to count as "major significance." |
| ***Zizi v. Cuccinelli***, No. 20-cv-07856, 2021 WL 2826713 (N.D. Cal. July 7, 2021) | Vacated EB-1A denial for a scientist-entrepreneur; held that USCIS unlawfully conflated Step 1 and Step 2 and improperly discounted patents used in regulated devices and venture/grant backing. |
| **Distinguishing *Amin v. Mayorkas***, 24 F.4th 383 (5th Cir. 2022) | *Amin* upheld denial where an engineer only showed internal value to his own employer. Always preempt *Amin* by highlighting **external, strictly independent adoption, citations, open-source usage, or industry-wide reliance** beyond the applicant's employer. |

## 4. Real-World Adjudication Landmines & Anti-Self-Sabotage Guardrails

1. **Zero Self-Sabotage in USCIS-Facing Prose:**
   - Never insert defensive CYA concessions into `01-petition-letter.md` or recommendation letters:
     - **No co-author overlap confessions:** Screen the 3-Tier Lifetime Co-Author Graph in `work/`, then present the strictly independent citations affirmatively in the brief without volunteering phrases like *"while there is overlapping collaboration"* or *"excluding collaborator overlap."*
     - **No equal-contributor or non-first-author self-demotions:** Present the applicant's exact architectural, experimental, or theoretical role affirmatively.
     - **No invented downgrade labels for peer review or awards:** Never label senior-scholar-routed or co-reviewed manuscripts as *"mentor-mediated evaluations"* or *"assisted reviews,"* and never label awards or finalist honors as *"contextual developmental recognitions."*
2. **Criterion (v) — Fuse Methodology Creation with the Scientific/Technical Breakthrough:**
   - A classic USCIS denial attack is to claim that the PI/advisor or company executive supplied the *"scientific direction/strategy"* while the applicant was merely an *"implementer / technician / tool builder."*
   - **Guardrail:** Never separate the scientific discovery or product breakthrough from the applicant's methodology, instrumentation, or system architecture. Prove that designing, building, modeling, or validating the experimental/computational methodology or instrument *is* the core advance that unlocked the capability and that independent peers cite and adopt.
3. **Criterion (viii) — Cross-Project Critical Role & Dual-Level Organizational Eminence:**
   - Never shrink an applicant's critical role to narrow task scheduling on a single sub-project when the record shows foundational, cross-project impact across the laboratory's or division's flagship outputs and funded programs.
   - Prove **both**: (a) how the applicant's technical leadership drove the establishment's core successes across its research/product portfolio and grants, and (b) the distinguished reputation of **both** the specific laboratory/division and its parent department/institution via competitive federal grants (NSF, NIH, DOE, DARPA, NSFC Key R&D), flagship publications, rankings, and independent media.
4. **Criterion (iv) Judging & Criterion (iii) Media Nuances:**
   - **Judging:** Only completed reviews count (invitations alone do not). Count a revision-round re-review as an additional judging act on the same manuscript, not a separate manuscript.
   - **Published Material:** Distinguish original third-party reporting from syndicated reprints—use syndicated reprints to demonstrate **dissemination reach** of a single source lineage, never to inflate the count of independent media outlets.
5. **Step 2 Final Merits — Evidence-Bounded Career-Stage Comparison When No Exact Percentile Exists:**
   - Never manufacture a fake "Top X%" claim from journal impact factors or raw counts without a verified peer-group denominator. When an exact percentile denominator is unavailable for a dimension, establish top-of-field standing through **convergent independent selections** (journal editors, program committees, award juries, funding agencies, and independent citing institutions worldwide).
